# Sales enablement best practices — secondary KB

Good sales enablement material is short enough that a rep actually reads it
before a call, and answers a small number of the questions they need most:

- What is being launched, and what does it solve for the buyer?
- Who is the target buyer (role, company type/size) — and just as useful,
  who is clearly *not* a fit, so reps don't waste time chasing bad leads.
- What are the 2–3 most common objections, and a one-line way to answer each
  — reps need something they can say out loud on a call, not a marketing
  tagline.
- What proof exists (a customer quote, a measurable result) that backs up
  the value message, so it doesn't read as an unsupported claim.
- What does the buyer need to already have in place (technical requirements,
  integrations) before this is a fit for them?

The most common failure mode in sales enablement is being too long (a
40-page product brief no one reads), too marketing-flavored (benefits
without proof), or missing the objection handling a rep actually needs
mid-conversation.
