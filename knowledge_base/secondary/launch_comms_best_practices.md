# Launch communications — secondary KB

Condensed reference for generating M1 (marketing email), M2 (user guide) and M3 (sales one-pager). Each point below is a short paraphrase; consult the linked source for detail.

## Strategy vs. plan

A launch *strategy* sets objectives, audience, positioning and messaging. A launch *plan* turns that into dated tasks, owners, channels and budget. Generate outputs against the plan layer, not the strategy layer.

## Components that must be present in generated content

- Target audience and ideal customer profile, defined by role and outcome rather than demographics alone
- Value proposition framed as customer outcome, not feature list
- Differentiation stated against named alternatives, including the status quo
- Channel fit — where the audience actually looks for information
- Success metric named explicitly

## Sequencing principle

Internal teams learn first, then leadership, then customer-facing teams, then customers, then public. A customer learning before the sales team is a process failure. This ordering drives what each generated artifact may contain and when.

## Audience-specific depth

Executives want business impact and risk. Managers want operational change. End users want how-to and time-to-value. The same feature brief should produce three materially different documents — this is the core differentiation test for M1/M2/M3.

## Common failure modes to avoid in generated output

- Generic content that could describe any product (a majority of marketers report their campaigns read as generic)
- Benefits stated without proof points
- Missing objection handling in sales material
- Inconsistent messaging across the three artifacts

## Metric categories for the "success measure" section

Revenue (MRR, deal size), efficiency (CAC, sales cycle length), quality (NPS, adoption rate, retention).
